## The Problem
Purdue Convocations wanted a clearer picture of how campus arts and live events connect to student engagement, academic performance, and sense of belonging—and how to turn one-time attendees into repeat ticket buyers.

## Key Results
* Analyzed **3+ years** of institutional and ticketing datasets (**50K+ rows each**) to uncover attendance and engagement patterns.
* Proposed a data-backed rewards program implementation projected to boost **customer retention by 25%** and **revenue by 68.6%**.

## Data
Multi-year Purdue Convocations attendance, student engagement, and related institutional datasets (50K+ rows per dataset; raw records kept private to respect partner confidentiality).

## Approach
1. Used **R**, **Python**, and **Google Sheets** to clean, standardize, and merge more than three years of multi-source data.
2. Evaluated relationships between event attendance, student engagement, academic performance, and sense of belonging.
3. Translated attendance frequency and drop-off patterns into a concrete rewards program structure designed to encourage repeat attendance and increase overall revenue.

## Analysis
Standardizing multiple years of ticketing and student records revealed clear differences between one-time event attendees and repeat patrons. Structuring incentives around that gap showed that moving even a modest share of occasional attendees into repeat engagement drives outsized gains in both retention and ticket revenue.

## What I Learned
Working with multi-year, 50K+ row operational datasets reinforced that cleaning and standardizing messy real-world records takes the bulk of the effort—and that the analysis only lands when you connect statistical patterns directly to an actionable business recommendation.
